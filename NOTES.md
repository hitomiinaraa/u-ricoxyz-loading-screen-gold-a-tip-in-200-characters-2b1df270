# Revision note

The line works because it turns a moment of dread into a small joke. A wall of
warnings usually reads as a scolding, and the reader is already braced for bad
news before the build finishes. Giving the warnings a personality flips that
expectation: instead of a list of faults, the reader meets something almost
charming, and the tension drops before the next line of output arrives.

That flip is what makes the tip land in the middle of a build. The audience is
tired, the screen has been scrolling for a while, and attention is thin. A joke
that needs no setup survives that moment, and this one needs none at all: the
reader already knows what warnings look like, so the comparison does its work
instantly.

The second half matters as much as the first. Stating plainly that nothing is
actually broken gives the reader permission to relax, which is the real job of
a loading screen: keep a stressed person company for a few seconds without
adding to the stress. The humour carries the reassurance instead of competing
with it.

## Final check

One last read-through before signing off: the wording still reads as one
continuous thought rather than two ideas stitched together. The first sentence
sets the scene, the second delivers the reassurance, and nothing in between
asks the reader to work for the joke. Leaving it exactly as it stands.
